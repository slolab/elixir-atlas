## **User stories**

### 1\. Steer the Node's focus

As a Head of Node or Node Coordinator, I want to see where my Node's research support is strong and where it has gaps, so that I can steer its focus and make the case to funders.

**Questions**:

1. Which research domains does our Node currently support, and through which services, people and projects?  
2. Where do user communities in our country engage with ELIXIR but our Node has little or no expertise?  
3. How does our Node's profile compare with other Nodes on theme X?  
4. Which of our people are single points of failure for a domain?

**A good answer contains**: a domain-by-domain summary of current activity, named people and services per domain, and a list of gaps with the evidence behind each.

### 2\. Write the grant

As a grant writer or work package lead, I want ELIXIR's competences on topic X consolidated in one place, so that I can build a credible work package/project and team quickly. I also want to make sure that I don’t reinvent the wheel and rebuild from what already exists. 

**Questions**:

1. Who in ELIXIR has expertise and track record on topic X, and at which Node?  
2. Which ELIXIR services, resources or training materials already address topic X?  
3. Which past ELIXIR bids covered topic X, which were funded?  
4. Which deliverables from earlier projects could we build on rather than duplicate?

**A good answer contain**s: a shortlist of people and Nodes with evidence of expertise, relevant services and outputs with links, and prior bids with outcome.

### 3\. Speak for ELIXIR

As a Node member preparing a talk, I want the current ELIXIR position, key messages and examples on theme X, so that I can represent ELIXIR accurately without starting from scratch.

Questions:

1. What is ELIXIR's current position on theme X, and where is it written down?  
2. What are three to five key messages and supporting examples or figures?  
3. Which existing slide decks or talks on theme X can I reuse?  
4. Which Node-level examples best illustrate theme X?

**A good answer contains**: a short position summary with its source and date, key messages, and links to reusable material.

### 4\. Streamline communities/platforms/focus group/working group/task force

As any ELIXIR-group co-lead or Hub community support officer, I want to see where there is overlap and who sits across several of them, so that we reduce duplication and avoid overloading the same people.

**Questions**:

1. Which groups work on overlapping topics or deliverables?  
2. Who holds roles in more than two places, and how much of their time does that represent?  
3. Where are two groups planning similar events or outputs this year?  
4. Which groups would benefit from a joint activity?

**A good answer contains**: an overlap map by topic and people, a list of people at risk of overload, and duplicated activities with dates. This can also help create better shaped staff exchange activities cutting across themes. 

### 5\. Get up to speed

As someone new to an ELIXIR role, I want a guided induction tailored to my role and Node, so that I understand who does what and where things live within weeks rather than months.

**Questions**:

1. What does someone in my role need to know in their first month?  
2. Who are my counterparts at the Hub and in other Nodes?  
3. Which groups, calls and mailing lists should I join?  
4. Where do I find the key documents, templates and processes for my role?

**A good answer contains**: a role-specific checklist, named counterparts, and links to the groups and documents to start with.

### 6\. Understand your community’s strategic landscape

As an ELIXIR Community co-lead or Hub community support officer, I want a consolidated picture of what my community does, who it overlaps with, and where it sits relative to the state of the art, so that I can set a credible roadmap, avoid duplication, and make the case for resources.

**Questions:**

1. What resources, tools, and training materials are supported or endorsed by community X and its members?  
2. Where does community X overlap with other communities in terms of topics, outputs, or shared members — and could those interfaces be strengthened?  
3. How much of community X’s meeting content, outputs, or plans is repetitive — within the community over time, or across communities working in the same space?  
4. Could community X serve as a use case for a Platform task, or conversely, which Platform activities is the community currently missing from?  
5. Given the state of the art in field X, what expertise or activity types appear underrepresented in community X’s track record?  
6. What are the key outputs of community X, and how do they connect to ELIXIR deliverables or external impact?  
7. What are community X’s plans for the next one to three years, and how do they align with the broader ELIXIR programme?

**A good answer contains:** a structured inventory of the community’s resources and people; an overlap map linking it to related communities and Platforms; a gap analysis against field-level expertise; a summary of key outputs with evidence of impact; and a forward-looking roadmap extracted or synthesised from community documents.

Offered by the Systems Biology Community: [https\://docs.google.com/document/d/1BdJVO\_5W3bywl8L5mLu66jRbZmjiWIPTsRjD6kNuejo/edit?pli=1\&tab=t.hv3pwkrzl7wt\#heading=h.xpipxuhte7ul](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1BdJVO_5W3bywl8L5mLu66jRbZmjiWIPTsRjD6kNuejo/edit?pli=1&tab=t.hv3pwkrzl7wt#heading=h.xpipxuhte7ul) 

### 7\. I want to find what a person has done in an ELIXIR project

The information on the ELIXIR Europe website contains details about the people that are involved in the project, and the Nodes, but data is not linked between Node and People, and I do not know which activities has the person in my Node been involved in. I may need it for reporting, to link work with someone else or simply to write a news story about it, or to report if we received funds or are only involved in-kind. 

**Questions**:

* Who in my Node was involved in this project?  
* Did they receive funding, or were they contributing in-kind?  
* What activity in this project were they involved in?

**A good answer contains**: a list of people connected to a Node, with what they have done, and if they managed to get funding support to run such an activity. Example of a project with limited amount of information about activity and funding: [https\://elixir-europe.org/how-we-work/scientific-programme/technology/24-compute](https://elixir-europe.org/how-we-work/scientific-programme/technology/24-compute) 

8\. Create and update an ELIXIR project webpage

As a Digital Content Editor at the ELIXIR Hub, I need to find and bring together information about an ELIXIR project, Commissioned Service or work package, so that I can create an accurate, clear and consistent webpage and keep it up to date as the activity progresses.

I currently use project plans and other sources to identify the information needed for a page. This can involve searching through documents, checking existing website content and asking colleagues to confirm details. When the page is published, I also need to know when information changes or new outputs become available.

**Questions:**

1\. What is the project about, and what are its aims and expected outcomes?

2\. Who leads the project, which Nodes are involved and how does it relate to ELIXIR Communities, Platforms or other activities?

3\. What are the project’s dates, planned activities, milestones and deliverables/outputs?

4\. Which related pages, resources, publications or outputs should be linked from the webpage?

5\. Where does each detail come from, and how current is it?

6\. Has anything changed since the webpage was created or last updated?

7\. Is any important information missing or inconsistent across the available sources?

8\. Which information is suitable for the public website, and what needs to be checked or approved before publication?

**A good answer contains:** a structured summary or draft webpage with the project’s purpose, expected outcomes, lead, participating Nodes, dates and relevant links. For an existing page, it should identify information that may need updating and any new outputs that could be added. It should show the source and date of key details, flag gaps or conflicting information and distinguish public information from material that needs checking before publication.

The Digital Content Editor can then review and adapt the content to the website’s style and structure, confirm any outstanding details with relevant colleagues and publish it through the usual process.

Example: [FAIR-FEGA project page](https://elixir-europe.org/how-we-work/scientific-programme/science/hdtr/fair-fega). This illustrates the type of page the Atlas could help create and maintain.

For the proof of concept, you could test both stages with the same example:

* Creating a page: Can the Atlas find the information needed to draft the page from approved sources?  
* Updating a page: Can it compare the published page with newer approved information and identify potential changes or outputs to add?

Project plans and other internal documents should only be used where the appropriate access and permission to process them have been confirmed.

### 10\. I’m managing the Communities of the ELIXIR and want to make informed decisions about their future

### Maintenance of data

A Coordinator role needs to access, clean and maintain data that is scattered across too many formats and places, verify it and compile it. This is additional work for the coordinator whose task does not involve cleaning a database, but presenting it to decision-makers, supporting HoNs or other scientific leads. The task is not followed if other things become a priority, even if maintaining the data will help the Node in the long term. 

For example, project plans live in Google Drive, managed by ELIXIR Hub. These documents are open to anyone with the link but it’s not public and is not intended to be public even if it reaches the wrong hands. We need to ensure that the results we provide also consider the access level offered. 

Information may live in the knowledge graph but how we display it and use it is restricted in other ways. One thing to note is that European Commission projects already display organisation-specific data for funding. E.g see ELIXIR STEERS in Cordis [https\://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101131096](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101131096)
