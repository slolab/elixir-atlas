## A – Story 4: where Communities overlap, stall or could learn from each other

The Communities Coordinator gets one view of overlaps, gaps and inactive areas across Communities, so she can work at a strategic level instead of chasing information. Co-leads and members get the right contact and channel without emailing every co-lead.

### Who it is for

- Primary: Katharina Heil, Communities Coordinator at the Hub. She agrees it would make the team's work easier and free time for strategic work.
- Secondary: Community co-leads and members; Platform, Focus Group and task force leads.

### What happens today

- Activities, future projects and approaches are shared only in Community meetings and live in rolling minutes, which nobody thinks to search.
- Slack is where people actually get answers, but only in the most active Communities.
- Most RDM Community members do not know the co-leads' mailing list is on the public Community page, so they email every co-lead in CC.
- RDM built an onboarding process for new co-leads and members when it formed, by choice rather than requirement. Other Communities have no way to find or reuse it.

### Questions

| # | Question | Sources |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| A1 | Which Communities work on overlapping topics, outputs or deliverables? | Community pages, F1000 ELIXIR Gateway, Zenodo, bio.tools collections |
| A2 | Which Communities or groups show no public activity in the last 12 months? | Community pages, news, events, publication dates |
| A3 | Who is the right contact or channel for Community X, and how do I join it? | Community pages (co-lead mailing lists, Slack links) |
| A4 | Which Communities have an onboarding process for co-leads or members, and what could others reuse? | Communities Handbook, Community pages, minutes |
| A5 | Who holds roles in more than two groups? | Co-lead lists (public), membership lists (internal) |
| A6 | Where are two groups planning similar events or outputs this year? | Rolling minutes, in-person meeting documents |
| A7 | How much of their time do people in overlapping roles commit? | Effort in Commissioned Service plans – leave out this week since the PMs are not published |

**A good answer contains**: an overlap map by topic and by people; thin or inactive areas, each with the date of its last evidence; for each Community, its contact route and whether it has onboarding; reusable practices such as RDM's onboarding, with a link.

**Demo this week**: run A1 to A4 across all Communities on public pages and ask Katharina or co-leads on site whether the overlaps and "inactive" flags are right.

**Out of scope**: Slack content, which is private and has no consent.

## B – Stories 7+8: what my HoN promised, and who is doing it

- Small Nodes: team members doing the work need to know what their Head of Node promised (worked example: ELIXIR Estonia). A Node finds every commitment its Head of Node makes in project plans, who in the Node delivers it, whether it is funded or in-kind and where it overlaps with other work – without needing the original link.
- ELIXIR Germany: writes an end-of-year report to its government every year and needs everything the Node delivered across projects.
- ELIXIR-UK: needs a complete picture of its projects for reporting and for decisions on next steps and national funding.

### Who it is for

- Primary: Heads of Node and Node Coordinators, where the role exists, and more informally given roles for smaller Nodes.
- Secondary: the Hub Digital Content Editor (story nine), who needs the same fields (Nodes, people, Communities, dates, outputs) for each Commissioned Service webpage.

### What happens today

- Project plans are open to anyone with the link but findable only through it, and links are shared in meetings with no single entry point.
- The Hub reorganises folders and changes access, so saved links break.
- Node Coordinator is not a mandatory role: Nodes without one miss the rolling minutes and updates sent to the coordinators' mailing list, and how to join it is not visible.
- Overlaps are hard to see and easy to forget: where was this mentioned, where can I check it again, who coordinates it? In practice, not the Hub.
- For an underfunded Node, knowing the overlaps is a funding issue as well as a design one.
- The Content Editor rebuilds each webpage from the plan by hand and learns about delays and new outputs only if colleagues remember to say.

### Questions

| # | Question | Sources |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| B1 | Which projects is my Node part of, current and past? | ELIXIR website project pages, CORDIS |
| B2 | What did my Node commit to deliver in project X, by when, and where is the plan? | Commissioned Service plans on Zenodo, Hub Drive archive |
| B3 | Who in my Node is involved, and in which activity? | Website people pages, project plans |
| B4 | Is my Node funded or contributing in-kind on project X? | CORDIS (e.g. ELIXIR STEERS); budgets stay redacted |
| B5 | Which other projects cover the same deliverable or topic, and where did I see it before? | Plans, website, Zenodo outputs |
| B6 | How do I join the Node Coordinators' mailing list and reach its rolling minutes? | Onboarding documents; minutes are internal |
| B7 | For each new Commissioned Service, what are its Nodes, people, Communities, dates and plan links? | Plans on Zenodo, website |
| B8 | Which outputs or date changes have appeared since a page was published? | Zenodo records linked to the plan |
| B9 | What did my Node deliver across all ELIXIR projects this year, with outputs and people? | Zenodo, publications, project plans |
| B10 | What is my Node's full project portfolio – current and ended, funded and in-kind – and where are the gaps? | Website, CORDIS, project plans |

**A good answer contains**: each commitment with its deliverable, date, people and a stable link to the plan (a Zenodo DOI where there is one); funded or in-kind where a public source says so; overlapping projects with the source of each; the route into coordinator channels; for national reporting, a year's or a whole portfolio's view by project, with outputs and funding status.

**Demo this week**: one small Node across its EC projects and the plans already on Zenodo, plus one draft webpage generated from a plan to show the Content Editor.

**Check with the Hub**: the Content Editor's account says the 2027–28 plans are on Zenodo. If current plans are too, B2 and B3 move to tier 1 for current work.

## C – Story 6: a Community's strategic landscape

Two routes run on the same Community data: a researcher finding what ELIXIR offers in their field (public, buildable now) and co-leads building a realistic roadmap and handing over to new leadership (public plus the documents Systems Biology has offered).

### C1 – Researcher route

**Who it is for**: researchers (not ELIXIR members) starting a project who want relevant tools, standards, best practice, training and the Communities working in their area.

**What happens today**: a Google search, then manual cross-checking across Community websites, bio.tools, FAIRsharing, TeSS, RDMkit and publications to work out what is officially recommended or community-supported and how Communities connect.

| # | Question (worked example: genome-scale metabolic modelling) | Sources |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| C1.1 | Which tools, standards, protocols and best practices should I consider? | bio.tools Community collections, FAIRsharing, RDMkit, Community publications |
| C1.2 | Which Community white papers or recommendations are relevant to my research? | Community landing pages, "Find out more" sections |
| C1.3 | Which training materials should I use? | TeSS |
| C1.4 | Which ELIXIR Communities work in this area, and who do I contact? | Community pages |

**A good answer contains**: resources grouped by type, each marked as officially recommended or community-supported, with links; the relevant Communities and how to reach them.

**Watch out**: this route answers "what does ELIXIR offer" rather than "who does what". It is the strongest public demo, but TeSS, FAIRsharing and bio.tools already search for resources. The Atlas adds value in the cross-referencing and in C1.4, so lead the demo with those.

### C2 – Co-lead route

**Who it is for**: Community co-leads, especially new ones, and members preparing Commissioned Service applications.

**What happens today**:

- Roadmaps take months of discussion and chasing members for links, and searches return results too broad to use because member interests are poorly mapped.
- White papers set roadmaps that are not realistic, and the Community loses momentum when plans cannot be met.
- New co-leads get one session with the Community liaison and a slide deck they soon forget.
- Buddying helps, but if all senior co-leads step down together, history that exists nowhere else goes with them.

| # | Question | Sources |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| C2.1 | Given our minutes, in-person meeting documents, members, supported resources and ELIXIR priorities, which tangible activities could our next roadmap take on? | Minutes, meeting documents, white papers, ELIXIR programme |
| C2.2 | Who are the relevant members, and which resources or tools do they support or develop? | bio.tools, member list |
| C2.3 | Which other Communities should we bring in as domain applications? | Community pages, publications |
| C2.4 | How are we progressing against the objectives in our current roadmap? | White paper objectives against minutes and outputs |
| C2.5 | Is a planned activity feasible with the people we have, compared with similar activities that succeeded elsewhere? | Other Communities' plans and delivered outputs |
| C2.6 | What does a new co-lead need to know that only the outgoing co-leads know? | Minutes, meeting documents, handover notes |

**A good answer contains**: an inventory of resources and people; progress against each objective with its evidence; feasibility flags with a comparable case; roadmap candidates linked to named members; a handover pack for new co-leads.

**Demo this week**: Systems Biology, using the minutes and in-person meeting documents the Community offered, with its co-leads checking the answers.
